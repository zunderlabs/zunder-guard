# Zunder Guard

A risk firewall between any trading bot or AI agent and Hyperliquid. The bot keeps its code and points its Hyperliquid client at Guard instead of the venue; Guard speaks Hyperliquid's own HTTP and WebSocket API, holds the real trading key (an API wallet, which cannot withdraw), checks every order against hard limits, sizes it down or refuses it, attaches a stop when the bot sent none, signs it again and sends it on. HIP-3 (builder-deployed) perpetual markets are opt-in per dex and supported in paper and testnet modes only. Guard runs locally; the browser relay is not part of this release.

Guard is **source-available** under the Elastic License 2.0 ("Fee, licence key and licence" below).

| Crate | What it is |
|---|---|
| `crates/zunder-guard-core` | No I/O; builds natively and for `wasm32-unknown-unknown`. Decoding, client authentication, the policy and the judge, re-signing, vetoes in Hyperliquid's shape, the licence check and issuing, the event schema. |
| `crates/zunder-guard` | The `zunder-guard` binary: configuration, setup, loopback HTTP/WebSocket server, persistent risk and decision journals, background synchronization and kill switch. It forwards client orders without running a trading strategy. |
| `crates/zunder-venue` | Shared venue foundations, without a trading session, executor or signer: Hyperliquid addresses, the network choice and mainnet consent, the HTTPS transport, instrument rules, and the persistent risk engine (`PersistentRisk`). |

## The public contract

What the MCP agent kit (`crates/zunder-guard-mcp`), the red-team suite (`tests/redteam`), the browser monitor and any bot build against. Everything else is internal and may change. Changes to this section are additive within a version.

Requests carrying an `Origin` that is not a loopback URL are refused with 403, and so are requests whose `Host` is not a loopback name (when Guard listens beyond loopback, in a container: not an IP address, `localhost`, a single-label name or a name under `.internal`, `.local` or `.localhost`): a web page can neither send to Guard from another site nor read its status through DNS rebinding. Bodies, WebSocket messages and frames over 1 MiB are refused; at most 8 WebSocket clients are served at once (HTTP 503 beyond).

### Signing with a client key

A bot signs exactly as for Hyperliquid, with the client key `zunder-guard init` or `zunder-guard pair` printed (a secp256k1 private key, `0x` and 64 hex digits) in place of an API wallet's: the L1 action scheme of the official SDKs. The action is encoded as MessagePack in Hyperliquid's field order, followed by the nonce (8 bytes big-endian), `0x00` (no vault), and, with `expiresAfter`, `0x00` and the expiry (8 bytes); its Keccak-256 is the `connectionId`; the EIP-712 phantom agent `Agent(string source,bytes32 connectionId)` in the domain `Exchange`, version `1`, chain id 1337, verifying contract zero, is signed. `source` may be `"a"` (mainnet) or `"b"` (testnet): Guard accepts both, since a client signature only authenticates the bot, and always signs what it forwards for the network it sends to. The request body is `{"action", "nonce", "signature": {"r", "s", "v"}, "vaultAddress": null, "expiresAfter": null | ms}` (the two optional fields may be left out). `vaultAddress` must be null.

The nonce (milliseconds, as the SDKs make it) must be above every nonce that client used before, within 30 s back and 5 s ahead of Guard's clock, and at or after Guard's start plus 5 s. Concurrent senders need one client key each (`pair`).

### Answers

- **Forwarded** (allow or resize): the venue's reply, with `response.data.statuses` in the bot's order and Guard's own orders (an attached stop) left out, plus Guard's `code` and `verdict` and the size asked and forwarded as fields of their own (`{"status":"ok","response":…,"code":"resized","verdict":"resize","requested_size":"10","size":"2.5537"}`: the entry's, or else the bot's first order's), so a resize is visible without reading the fill sizes. `resize` also means a change that is not a size (an attached stop, a widened stop limit): compare the sizes.
- **Veto**: Hyperliquid's error shape, which the SDKs and ccxt raise with the text, plus the reason code as a field of its own: `{"status":"err","code":"<code>","response":"Zunder Guard veto [<code>]: <text>"}`.
- **Paper mode**: `{"status":"err","code":"<code>","verdict":"allow|resize|veto","requested_size":…,"size":…,"response":"Zunder Guard paper mode: would <allow|resize|veto> [<code>]: <text> (nothing was sent)"}` (the sizes for an entry).

Guard never forwards a larger size than the bot asked for: every forwarded order of the bot's is at most its requested size (the verdict is allow, a resize down, or a veto). The reason codes are listed under "Events".

### Events

Every event is one JSON object `{seq, at_ms, kind, …}`, journaled (synced) before anything is sent for it and served by `/guard/events`. Schema version 1 (`event.rs`): fields may be added, never removed or changed in meaning.

## One request

1. **Kill file and decode** (`action.rs`). Accepted: `order` (groupings `na`, `normalTpsl`, `positionTpsl`; a new order whose client id starts `0x7a67`, the prefix of Guard's own stops, is refused as `invalid`), `cancel`, `cancelByCloid`, `modify`, `batchModify`, `updateLeverage`, `updateIsolatedMargin`, `scheduleCancel`. Every other action is refused by name; transfers, withdrawals, `approveAgent`, `approveBuilderFee`, `setReferrer`, vault and staking actions get their own reason (`funds_or_permissions`). Decoding is strict (unknown fields, floats for integers, numbers for strings, malformed prices refused), at most 64 orders per action.
2. **Authenticate** (`auth.rs`): the signer recovered exactly as Hyperliquid does must be a client; the nonce rules above. The nonce is consumed once the signature checks out, so a vetoed request cannot pass on replay.
3. **Read the account**: from the account stream when it is clean ("The account stream" below: no request to the venue at all), else fresh for the request: `clearinghouseState`, `frontendOpenOrders`, `allMids` concurrently, for the main dex and, with the `dex` parameter, for every HIP-3 dex the markets name, all at once (so a transfer between two of them is not seen half done), merged into one view; `meta` (each dex's) and the account mode (`userAbstraction`) cached for a minute, `perpDexs` for ten, each HIP-3 dex's `perpsAtOpenInterestCap` for one; for a HIP-3 entry also its coin's book (`l2Book`, aggregated: "HIP-3 markets"); within the request-read budget above. When it cannot be read (or the budget is spent: `rate_limited`), only an action of reduce-only orders goes on, unjudged and as sent (its market stops are not widened, so they do not count as protection; Guard's own stop follows at the next protection round); everything else is refused.
4. **Risk engine**: `PersistentRisk::observe_view` shows `zunder-risk` the equity and the positions with the stops that cover them, written before anything else; `settled` only when Guard heard back from the venue more than 5 s ago. A halt or the kill switch flattens. In the background sync (every `sync_seconds`, at least 5), when settled (and at least every 30 s regardless, and right after Guard cancelled an entry that may have filled in part) and not halted, every position no market stop covers in full gets Guard's own stop at the tighter of the default stop distance from the mid and the stop the risk engine recorded for it (`protect` event). A recorded stop the price has already gone through is never widened: Guard closes the position instead. A stop whose worst fill lies beyond the position's liquidation price is placed and reported as an alert; Guard's own stop resting without counting (the venue reporting its limit within 5% of its trigger) is reported and not stacked again. If the venue refuses the stop, Guard closes the position; when its answer is lost or unreadable, Guard first looks for the stop among the open orders by its client id.
5. **Judge** (`judge.rs`). 6. **Journal** the decision, synced; if it cannot be written, nothing is sent.
7. **Send** (testnet or mainnet): isolated leverage first when a new position needs it, confirmed by reading `activeAssetData` back (else nothing else is sent); not when the account stream showed the coin set to isolated at that leverage or less within the last 1.5 s and after the account last changed (kept: less leverage puts the liquidation further away); then the action with a fresh Guard nonce and an expiry of at most 30 s; when an entry filled but every stop sent with it was refused, a reduce-only IOC close at once, checked from its fill status (`error` event and `alert`); when the entry rests but its stop was refused, the entry is cancelled; a filled, protected entry is recorded with the risk engine (`PersistentRisk::record_entry`); Guard's own stop is cancelled only once the bot's replacing stop rests.

## The rules

One source of truth: `Policy` in `crates/zunder-guard-core/src/policy.rs` (its `…_BOUNDS` constants); the rules crate (`deploy/guard/rules`, read by the binary for `zr1_` codes), its TypeScript twin and `schema-v1.json` take defaults and bounds from it, and a test keeps them equal. Five rules are the risk engine's own limits (`Policy::risk_limits`); the defaults are Guard's own preset (`Policy::guard_defaults()`), not read from `RiskLimits::default()` (the limits of Zunder's own bot), though equal in value today. On mainnet a policy may not be looser than `Policy::mainnet_ceiling()`: the bot's frame for the engine's five and Guard's own rules pinned separately, so loosening Guard's defaults never loosens mainnet.

| Rule | Default | Bounds | Enforced by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Max leverage | 5x | above 0, at most 10 (four decimals) | engine (total value over equity); the isolated leverage Guard sets or allows is the whole number below, at least 1x |
| Max loss at the stop | 2% of equity | above 0, at most 5% | engine: size = budget / (distance to the stop + fees and slippage both ways) |
| Protective stop | required: `attach` (or `refuse`) | | judge |
| Default stop distance | 2% of the price | above 0, at most 50%, below the min liquidation distance | judge |
| Min distance to liquidation | 10% of the price | 1% to 50%, above the default stop distance | judge |
| Max position | 200% of equity | above 0, at most 1000% and max leverage | judge |
| Max open risk | 6% of equity | at least one trade's, at most 20% | engine |
| Daily loss stop | 6% | above 0, at most 15% | engine, persisted |
| Drawdown halt | 25% | above 0, at most 50% | engine, persisted; only a person's review clears it |
| Markets | every market of the main dex (`*`) | `"all"` or up to 32 entries: names (letters, digits and `: / @ . _ -`), `*` (every main-dex market), `dex:*` (every market of that HIP-3 dex); HIP-3 markets only by name, at most 2 HIP-3 dexes, none on mainnet | judge |

These are the rules schema v1's bounds (the upper ones are `RiskLimits::aggressive()`); percentages have at most four decimal places, as a rules code can carry them.

Also in the policy: `entry_price_bound` 0.5% (a limit further beyond the mid is pulled in to it, so the worst fill is known), `fee_bps` 4.5 and `slippage_bps` 1 (per side, counted into each entry's risk), `stop_slippage` 10% (5% to 20%: the limit of Guard's market stops, Hyperliquid's stop-market tolerance as in Zunder's own executor; the liquidation must lie beyond the stop's fill at that slippage), `exit_slippage` 5% (Guard's own closing orders), and `max_trading_equity_usd` (the equity cap, off by default, at most 2,500). Out-of-bounds values are refused, never clamped. **On mainnet** no rule may be looser than the mainnet ceiling (`Policy::within_mainnet_ceiling`: Zunder's default risk frame for the engine's five, Guard's own rules pinned at today's defaults, independent of Guard's default preset), and the equity cap is required.

### An entry

An entry is an order that is not reduce-only and does not close an opposite position: one per action, a limit order (an IOC limit is how the SDKs and ccxt send market orders), with or without its TP/SL. Judged in this order; the first refusal wins:

1. The kill switch, the daily loss stop and the drawdown halt refuse every entry.
2. The market: a perp of the main dex or of a HIP-3 dex the markets name (asset 100000 + 10000 × dex + index), listed, not delisted (on a HIP-3 dex: not halted, `market_halted`), in the policy's markets; on a HIP-3 dex also margined in USDC and not at its open-interest cap (`open_interest_cap`). A HIP-3 dex the markets do not name (`dex_not_allowed`), spot (asset 10000 + index) and HIP-4 outcomes (100000000 + 10 × outcome + side) are refused for every order, modify, `updateLeverage` and `updateIsolatedMargin`, reduce-only or not; only cancels go on any asset.
3. The account: standard mode with a positive equity (the perp account's value; spot balances left out, which only makes sizes smaller), and a mid price.
4. The worst fill: the limit price, pulled in to `entry_price_bound` beyond the mid.
5. The stop: the entry's own stop loss (a `normalTpsl` child, or a reduce-only stop loss in the same action), or under `attach` one Guard puts at the default stop distance from the lower of fill and mid (the higher for a short), rounded towards the price; under `refuse`, no stop is a veto. Only a market stop counts: a stop-limit can trigger and then rest unfilled on the wrong side of a falling market, so it is forwarded (it only reduces) but protects nothing, replaces nothing, and an entry whose only stop is a stop-limit is an entry without a stop. A market stop's limit (`p`) caps its fill too, so Guard widens the limit of every market stop it forwards (in an order, a modify or a batch) to `stop_slippage` (10%) beyond the trigger where the bot's lies closer, as a resize; on the venue, a market stop counts as protection only when its limit lies at least 5% beyond its trigger. A limit the bot set wider than `stop_slippage` is kept: it fills in a larger gap, at a price beyond what the leverage check assumed (narrowing it would risk not filling at all). A stop at or through the mid is refused.
6. Size, by `RiskEngine::size_entry`: loss at the stop within the per-trade budget, open risk within its budget, total value within the leverage cap. Open risk counts every position (the engine's record or the venue's, the larger) at the stops that cover it (several stops sized to separate entries cover a position together, summed from the tightest outwards), and every resting entry at its limit and waiting stop. A position or resting entry without a stop refuses every entry.
7. The position cap: the coin's position plus resting entries on the same side, at the higher of their price and the mid.
8. Liquidation. A new position gets isolated margin at the highest whole leverage within the caps whose liquidation lies beyond the stop's worst fill by 10% of that distance plus 5% of the price, and at least the min distance away (the formula of Zunder's own executor), and that also suits every entry already resting on the coin; Guard sends that `updateLeverage` first. An existing position must already be isolated within the caps, its liquidation clear of both the new stop and the stop that covers it now; Guard never changes an open position's leverage.
9. The quantity: the smallest of the bot's request, the engine's size, the position cap and the bot's own stop's size, and on a HIP-3 dex what its margin account can fund and what its book can take ("HIP-3 markets"), rounded down; the value at the stop must reach the venue's 10 USDC minimum.

The forwarded entry keeps the bot's client order id; its TP/SL children are cut to the forwarded quantity (never enlarged); Guard's attached stop carries a client order id starting `0x7a67` (`zg`) followed by a hash that includes a secret of the Guard process, so a bot cannot predict it.

### Stops only tighten

- A bot's later stop for a position Guard protects (Freqtrade places its own after the fill) is forwarded; when it stands on its own (grouping `na`), is a market stop, covers the whole position and is tighter than Guard's, Guard cancels its own once the bot's rests. Otherwise Guard's stays.
- A modify of a stop: only into a reduce-only market stop loss, at least as tight, no smaller (a position TP/SL keeps covering the whole position), within the open-risk budget. A stop waiting for its entry stays on the losing side of the entry's price.
- A cancel: Guard's own stop cannot be cancelled while its position is open. Another stop may go only if the position stays covered and open risk within budget; the stop of a resting entry goes only with the entry.
- A modify of a resting entry is refused (`modify_entry`): whether the venue keeps the stop waiting for it through a modify is not established. Cancel it and send a new entry.
- `scheduleCancel` may be cleared, never set. `updateLeverage` must be isolated, within the caps, may only lower an open position's leverage, and must suit every entry resting on the coin. `updateIsolatedMargin` may only add margin.
- Reduce-only orders, closes (an opposite order no larger than the position is sent reduce-only) and cancels work while halted or killed. A reduce-only order on its own (grouping `na`) larger than the position it reduces is cut to the position's size. A client's own `builder` field is refused on an entry (`client_builder`) and removed from an exit; only Guard's appears, on the order actions Guard sends while the fee is approved ("Fee, licence key and licence").

## HIP-3 markets

HIP-3 lets builders deploy perpetual markets on Hyperliquid. Guard supports these markets in paper and testnet modes only; mainnet refuses every HIP-3 market (`Policy::within_mainnet_ceiling`).

### The venue, as verified on 6 Oct 2026

Each from Hyperliquid's documentation ("Asset IDs", "Info endpoint" and its "Perpetuals" page, "HIP-3: builder-deployed perpetuals", "Rate limits") and checked against the public API (mainnet and testnet, under 150 of request weight a minute):

- **Asset ids**: `100000 + 10000 × dex + index`, `dex` the dex's position in `perpDexs` (the main dex is 0, `null` in the list, and has no such ids), `index` the coin's in that dex's `meta` (the docs' example: `test:ABC`, dex 1, index 0, is 110000; on testnet `xyz` is dex 65, so its first coin is 750000). Outcome ids start at 100,000,000, so a HIP-3 dex index above 9,989 is refused rather than read.
- **Names**: `dex:COIN` everywhere (`xyz:GOLD`), in `meta`, positions, orders, mids and fills.
- **One margin account per dex**: `meta`, `clearinghouseState`, `frontendOpenOrders` and `allMids` take a `dex` parameter; without it they answer for the main dex only (checked on accounts holding both: the main dex's answers carry no `xyz:` coin, `xyz`'s nothing else). Each dex has its own `accountValue` and `withdrawable` in standard account mode; money moves between them only by a transfer.
- **`perpDexs`**: `[null, {name, fullName, deployer, oracleUpdater, feeRecipient, assetToStreamingOiCap, subDeployers, …}, …]`; 11 entries on mainnet (10 HIP-3 dexes), 268 on testnet.
- **Margin**: `meta` gives `marginMode` per coin: `noCross` (isolated only, 102 of xyz's 131 coins), `strictIsolated` (isolated, and margin cannot be removed), or none (cross allowed); `onlyIsolated` is the older flag for both. Guard keeps every position isolated and never removes margin, so all three fit; it refuses cross (`cross_margin`) as on the main dex.
- **Collateral**: `meta.collateralToken`: USDC (0) on xyz, para, mkts, io, abcd; USDH (360), USDE (235) or USDT0 (268) on flx, vntl, km, hyna and cash, whose markets are all delisted. Guard sums equity in USDC and opens nothing on another collateral (`unsupported_market`).
- **Open-interest caps**: per coin in `perpDexs` (`assetToStreamingOiCap`) and `perpDexLimits` (notional); `perpsAtOpenInterestCap` with `dex` lists the coins at their cap (weight 20).
- **Oracle and halts**: the deployer sets the oracle (the mark that triggers stops) and can halt a market with `haltTrading`, which cancels its orders and settles its positions at the mark; it can resume it. A halted or settled market shows as `isDelisted` in its dex's `meta` (vntl's 15 markets after Ventuals wound down). Inferred from that, not seen at the moment of a halt.
- **Fees**: with the deployer's fee scale `s` (`deployerFeeScale` in `meta`, at most 3), a taker pays `(1 + s) × 4.5` bp below 1 and `2s × 4.5` from 1 up, a tenth in growth mode.
- **Books**: `l2Book` takes `nSigFigs` (2 to 5): bids are rounded down to the figures, asks up, 20 levels a side (xyz:GOLD at 2 figures: levels 100 wide, 20 of them 50% of the price).
- **Weights**: `clearinghouseState`, `allMids` and `l2Book` 2; `meta`, `perpDexs`, `frontendOpenOrders`, `perpsAtOpenInterestCap` 20; nothing extra for the `dex` parameter.
- **Testnet liquidity**: `xyz` (dex 65, 70 coins, USDC) has books, thin: xyz:GOLD showed 0.05 at the best levels and about $2,300 of daily volume; most of its coins have no mid at all.

### What Guard does

- **Opt-in per dex.** The markets name a HIP-3 dex by `dex:*` (every market of it) or `dex:COIN` (one); `*` stays every market of the main dex and never a HIP-3 one, so the default is unchanged. Guard reads, judges and protects the main dex and the named dexes (at most two), and nothing else. The rules code stays `zr1_`: a code written before decodes exactly as it did (`*` never allowed a HIP-3 market, which earlier versions refused), and the two new forms, `dex:*` and `*` beside HIP-3 entries, were refused by every earlier decoder, so an older Guard refuses such a code rather than misreading it. A code that names a HIP-3 coin and was valid before (`["BTC", "xyz:XYZ100"]`) now lets that coin trade on paper and testnet.
- **Account-wide.** Equity is the sum of the USDC accounts of the dexes Guard reads. The risk engine's daily loss stop, drawdown halt, open risk and leverage cap, and the position cap, measure that sum: a loss on a HIP-3 dex counts toward the daily stop as one on the main dex, and an unprotected HIP-3 position blocks entries anywhere. A transfer between two dexes Guard reads changes nothing; one to a dex it does not read counts as a loss (as spot balances, left out). So does removing a dex from the markets: its account drops out of the equity, which can fire the daily stop or the drawdown halt; close and move what is there first.
- **Per dex.** The isolated leverage Guard sets is bounded by that coin's own `maxLeverage` (its dex's `meta`), the liquidation distance by the same formula as on the main dex (margin tiers are not modelled, as there). Each HIP-3 entry is also cut to what its dex's own margin account can fund at that leverage (`withdrawable` × leverage / worst fill; `dex_margin` when that is below the venue's minimum: move USDC to the dex first). Its fee is the dex's (`fee_bps` × the fee multiple above, growth mode's discount not counted, the most a deployer may set when `meta` does not say), so the loss at the stop stays within budget.
- **Thin books.** A HIP-3 entry is cut so that the position, with what already rests on its side, is at most half (`HIP3_BOOK_SHARE`) of the depth its stop can fill into: the levels on the side the stop trades against that lie wholly between its trigger and its worst fill, read at 2 significant figures (3 where the price's leading digits are below 4, so that 20 levels reach the worst fill); what rests nearer the price will have traded by the time the stop fires, what lies beyond the twentieth level counts as none. No book read refuses (`thin_book`). One `l2Book` read (weight 2) per HIP-3 entry; a preview reads it from the `/info` passthrough budget.
- **Halts and caps.** A halted market refuses entries (`market_halted`); Guard places no stop on it and sends no close (the venue settles), and reports it. A coin at its cap refuses entries (`open_interest_cap`; the caps are reused for a minute, so a coin can meet its cap in between: the venue then refuses the order).
- **Protection.** The background sync protects and, on a halt or the kill switch, flattens the positions of every dex Guard reads, each dex's orders in actions of their own. An unreadable account lets reduce-only orders on those dexes through unjudged, as on the main dex.
- **Dexes the rules do not name.** Nothing goes there but cancels (`dex_not_allowed` for orders, modifies, `updateLeverage` and `updateIsolatedMargin`). The sync looks at one such dex every 30 s, in turn (`clearinghouseState`, weight 2; about 5 minutes for mainnet's 10, 2.2 hours for testnet's 267), and reports positions it finds in the status and as an alert. It never touches them, and neither does the kill switch: those positions were made outside Guard, perhaps a person's hedge, and Guard holds no mandate there; the person closes them.
- **Budget.** Each HIP-3 dex adds a read of 24 to every request and every sync and 40 a minute of cached reads; the sync slows down to keep its share (`sync_interval_ms`), and fewer requests a minute are judged (8.4 a minute on average with one dex, its book included).

### Gaps

- A deployer's oracle moves are not detected; stops trigger on the mark it sets.
- Book depth is a snapshot: the book at the moment a stop fires may be thinner. Liquidity beyond 20 aggregated levels is not counted.
- Growth mode's fee discount is not counted (sizes are smaller than they could be).
- Margin tiers (`marginTables`) are not modelled, as on the main dex.
- A transfer between dexes that lands between two reads of one request could be seen half done; the reads go out together, so this needs the venue to answer them from different blocks, but the effect is large when it happens: 2,500 of 10,000 moved from the main dex to xyz seen as a loss is a 25% drawdown (the halt fires: flatten, a person's review); seen as a gain it raises the peak to 12,500 for good, so the drawdown halt then fires after a 6.25% real loss. Move money between dexes while no bot trades, or expect a halt.
- Adding a dex (or moving money onto one Guard reads) in the middle of a day counts its account as a gain against the day's start, so the daily stop allows a larger loss that day; removing one counts as a loss (above). The drawdown peak keeps the larger figure.
- Asset ids rest on a dex keeping its index in `perpDexs` (the list only grows; Guard re-reads it every ten minutes and every coin's name must carry the dex's prefix, but an index that changed would not be noticed within those ten minutes).
- A HIP-3 dex Guard cannot read (its `meta`, its caps or its answers) is left out of the background sync, which then protects and flattens what it did read but shows the risk engine nothing (the missing account would read as a loss) and judges no entry until the dex reads again; its positions go without Guard's protection meanwhile (an alert says so).
- The agent kit (`zunder-guard-mcp`) reads the main dex's `meta` only and places no HIP-3 orders.

## Threat model

What Guard protects against: a bot or agent, buggy or compromised, that holds only its client key. It cannot withdraw or move funds (refused; the API wallet cannot either), approve agents or builder fees, open beyond the size, open-risk, leverage and position limits, trade without a stop that can fill, loosen or strip stops, trade after a daily stop, a drawdown halt or the kill switch, replay or forge requests, or make Guard forward more than it asked for.

What it does not protect against:

- **The machine.** Whoever controls the machine can read the API wallet key (in memory, in the systemd credential or the key file) and trade with it directly. Run Guard as its own user, on a machine the bot does not control if possible.
- **Losses within the limits.** A gap through a stop loses more than its distance; a market stop can fill up to 10% beyond its trigger (`stop_slippage`); a bot's stop-limit may not fill at all in a fast market, which is why it never counts as a stop. The limits bound what is risked by design, not what can happen.
- **Trades into illiquid markets** that drain value through the spread (the 3Commas pattern): the market list narrows this; `all` does not. On a HIP-3 dex the book cap keeps a position within what its stop can fill into, not the spread paid on the way in.
- **A HIP-3 deployer** sets its markets' oracle (the mark that triggers stops), leverage limits and fees, and can halt a market, settling every position at the mark. Guard refuses entries on a halted market and counts a deployer's fee scale into the risk, but a deployer who moves the oracle can push a position through its stop or to liquidation; the markets list is where a user decides which deployers to trust.
- **Positions and orders made elsewhere** (the web app, another key): Guard counts them and refuses entries while one lacks a stop, but does not stop them being made.
- **The venue's answers** are trusted, read over HTTPS from the network's own URL.
- **Guard down**: no new orders; positions keep their stops on the venue.
- **The decision journal** detects changed, missing or reordered lines before the last point it knows was on disk; after it, what a crash may leave is moved aside (`decisions*.jsonl.cut-<ms>`), never deleted (its chain has no key), as with the risk journal.
- **Local processes** can reach the port; without a client signature they can only read the status, preview, and pass info requests (rate-limited). A web page on another site is refused.

### Several Guards on one IP address

Hyperliquid allows 1,200 of request weight a minute per IP address, and a Guard alone sizes its budgets for all of it (the `/info` row above). **Running several Guards on one machine (one account each)? Give each `ip_share = 1/N`** as a decimal (`"0.5"` for two, `"0.3333"` for three), every one of them, the one that was there first included (`init --ip-share`, or `ip_share` in `guard.toml`; `run --ip-share` and `ZUNDER_GUARD_IP_SHARE` override it for that run only, and `run` says so; `init --force` keeps the old config's share unless given a new one). Their worst minutes then add up to at most 1,200, and each keeps its own reserve for protection.

How a share is spent (`crates/zunder-guard/src/budget.rs`): `1,200 × ip_share` a minute, of which first what Guard reads on a timer whatever the share (the sync's cached reads, 46 a minute and 40 more per HIP-3 dex; one extra read of every dex, 24 a dex; the builder-fee check while unapproved, 20). Every other budget (the sync's reads, the bots' request budget, the two reduce-only allowances, the passthrough, the protection reserve) gets the same fraction of its share-1 size: `(1,200 × ip_share − fixed) / (1,200 − fixed)`. A burst keeps at least the largest single thing it pays for (the request budget a read of every dex with an entry and its leverage: 46, 72 or 96; the passthrough a `userRole`, 60; an allowance its share-1 burst, 5), and its refill gives up the difference. The sync reads less often instead. Protection's own sends and the sync's reads come from no bucket, so a bot's spent budget never holds them up.

Guard refuses to start (and `init` and `check-config` refuse the config) when the share would stretch the sync beyond 30 s between two reads (the longest Guard waits to protect a position while the account keeps changing), leave the request budget less than one account read a minute, or leave less than 20 + 3 a dex a minute for protection (a stop looked up by its client id; a cancel, a stop and a close per dex). A round that a send overtook reads twice and skips the next round; where the share stretched the sync beyond 15 s, that would leave more than 30 s, so such a Guard's sync reads holding Guard's lock (one read a round, never skipped; a request arriving meanwhile waits for that read, usually one round trip, at most 5 s, once a round; `budgets.sync_reads_locked`). A locked read the venue does not answer within 5 s ends its round, and the next round reads without the lock, as at share 1: the 30 s hold while the venue answers within 5 s; on a venue slower than that, good reads may come only every other round (36 s at 0.3333 with the main dex alone, 60 s at 0.466 with one HIP-3 dex). A `sync_seconds` above 15 may still leave longer, as at share 1: that is the config's choice, not the share's. With today's weights:

| | smallest share | Guards per IP | at `ip_share = 0.5` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main dex alone | 0.291 | 3 | sync every 10.9 s; requests 1.53 a second, a burst of 46; each allowance 0.414 a second, 5; passthrough 0.927 a second, 73; reserve 51 a minute |
| One HIP-3 dex | 0.466 | 2 | sync every 27.4 s, holding the lock; requests 1.045 a second, 72; allowances 0.378, 5; passthrough 0.856, 68; reserve 31 |
| Two HIP-3 dexes | 0.749 | 1 | refused |

`/guard/status` reports `ip_share` and `budgets` (every bucket, the sync's interval and the reserve), `zunder-guard status` one line of it, and `run` prints them at start when the share is below 1 or comes from `--ip-share` or the environment rather than the config. `zunder-guard kill` reads the config file's share for the interval it reports. Each start spends about 100 outside the budgets (the key's `userRole`, 60, the account mode, `perpDexs`) and starts every bucket full: start several Guards a few seconds apart, not all at once after a reboot. The share covers Guard's own requests only: a bot that also calls Hyperliquid directly, or the website's browser tools (up to 600 request weight a minute), spend the same address's 1,200, so leave them room in the shares. Hyperliquid's WebSocket limits per address apply as well (among them 10 unique users across user subscriptions; each Guard's account stream is one).

The website hands a person their settings as `zr1_…`: rules schema v1, shared with the website, the docs and the CLI (`deploy/guard/rules`, with its TypeScript twin and test vectors). `zunder-guard config get rules` prints a config's code. Its defaults and bounds are this policy's.

A key file must not be writable by anyone else; on bare metal it must be readable by its owner only (0600); in a container (`--container` or `ZUNDER_GUARD_CONTAINER`, or Docker's `/.dockerenv`; Podman and others pass the flag), where platform secret files are often world-readable and Guard is the only user, a readable file is accepted with a warning. A mainnet key comes on standard input only, as for Zunder's runner, so platforms that offer only environment variables (Railway) stay paper and testnet. On an empty home with `ZUNDER_GUARD_RULES` and `ZUNDER_GUARD_ACCOUNT` set, `run` first sets up paper mode non-interactively; sending modes need `init`. SIGTERM and SIGINT stop it cleanly, also as PID 1.

## Setup

`zunder-guard init` (interactive by default, on a terminal; `--rules zr1_…` to start from the website's rules):

1. shows the rules and asks to keep or edit them, value by value within the bounds;
2. asks for the account address;
3. asks for the mode: paper (default; then which network's account it reads, mainnet by default), testnet, or mainnet typed in full, then the account address again and the equity cap (no rule looser than the mainnet ceiling);
4. for testnet or mainnet, unless `--no-key`: asks for the API wallet key without echo (the binary switches echo off itself; the image has no `stty`), never shown, logged or written in plain text by Guard; checks with the venue that it is an API wallet of the account, which cannot withdraw (the main wallet's own key is refused);
5. generates a client key for the bot from the operating system's randomness, shown once; only its address is kept;
6. generates a pairing code for the browser monitor, shown once; only its SHA3-256 is kept;
7. checks the config;
8. stores the key: with `systemd-creds encrypt` where available (`/etc/credstore.encrypted/zunder-guard-key`, encrypted with the machine's key and its TPM where there is one), otherwise in `api-wallet-key` next to the config, mode 0600, with a warning that it lies there in plain text; then writes the config;
9. starts the risk journal for paper or testnet at the account's equity now (setting up is the person's decision to start one); mainnet's waits for the person's confirmation (`ZUNDER_MAINNET_CONFIRM=<account> zunder-guard journal-init --mode mainnet`);
10. prints the next steps.

Without a terminal it refuses unless `--non-interactive` is given: the answers then come from the flags and `ZUNDER_GUARD_*`, the key from standard input (`--key-stdin`) unless `--no-key`.

**Mainnet as a systemd service** (`deploy/guard/install.sh`, `deploy/guard/README.md`, "Mainnet under systemd"): the key is a `systemd-creds` encrypted credential that the unit hands to `run --key-stdin` on standard input, so the binary's "standard input only" rule holds and the key is never in plain text on disk, in an argument or in the environment. The installer requires `systemd-creds` for mainnet (no plain-file fallback, refused before the key is asked for), enables the unit without starting it, and leaves `journal-init --mode mainnet` and the first start to the person. A one-shot unit fed by a person at every start was weighed and refused: after a crash or a reboot positions would stay without Guard until someone typed the key again.

## Windows

Guard 1.0 runs natively on Windows (x86_64): paper and testnet. Mainnet is refused there, by
`init` and by `run`: a mainnet key comes on standard input only, and Windows has no equivalent
of the systemd service that hands it over from an encrypted credential at every start.

- **Home** `%LOCALAPPDATA%\zunder-guard` (journals, config, kill file), unless
  `ZUNDER_GUARD_HOME` says otherwise.
- **The testnet key**: `init` asks for it with hidden input and keeps it in the Windows
  Credential Manager (DPAPI, this user; service `zunder-guard`, named by the configuration's
  folder). `run --network testnet` reads it there itself; `--key-stdin` works as on Unix.
- **Key files** (`client add --out`, `--key-file`, the MCP kit's `--key-file`) are owner-only by
  ACL (`zunder_venue::owner_only`): created with the inherited entries removed and only the user
  granted; read only when the ACL allows no one but the user, the owner, SYSTEM and
  Administrators.
- **Stopping**: Ctrl-C, Ctrl-Break, closing the console, logoff and shutdown, as SIGTERM and
  SIGINT on Unix. No service in 1.0; Guard runs in the foreground.
- **Installing**: `deploy/guard/README.md`, "Windows" (`i.ps1`, winget).

## Paper, testnet, mainnet

- **Paper**: reads the account and prices of the config's network through an `/info`-only client, judges and journals every request, never sends; it has no way to. Its own risk journal (`risk-paper.jsonl`, scope `paper-<network>`) and decision journal.
- **Testnet** (`run --network testnet`): a testnet config in testnet mode; the key's address must be the config's `api_wallet` and an API wallet of the account; the account in standard mode; the risk journal scoped to testnet and the account.
- **Mainnet** (`run --network mainnet --key-stdin`): the existing mainnet guards, nothing weaker: a mainnet config in mainnet mode with `allow_mainnet`, the equity cap, the account and the API wallet, and no rule looser than the mainnet ceiling (`check_mainnet`; refused by the testnet command and the other way round); `ZUNDER_MAINNET_CONFIRM` naming the account at this start (`MainnetConsent::from_env`, the only way to a mainnet `VenueNetwork`, from which the HTTPS transport and the signing network are built); a risk journal started for mainnet and this account; all checked before the key is read; then the key's address and role. Live mainnet validation is pending. Enabling mainnet requires explicit operator consent.

The kill switch: `zunder-guard kill --reason "…"` writes `kill` in the state directory (read leniently from a config that no longer validates; with a warning when no Guard answers on the configured address). Guard latches it at start, on every request and at every sync, readable or not: it opens nothing and flattens (cancels every order that could open a position, closes every position with a reduce-only IOC order 5% beyond the mid). It stays latched until a person removes the file and restarts Guard. A daily loss stop or drawdown halt flattens the same way, from the positions alone when the rest of the account cannot be read; in paper mode the flatten is journaled, not sent.

## Journals

- **Risk journal**: `zunder-venue`'s `PersistentRisk`, scoped to network and account. Guard adds two venue-neutral calls: `observe_view` (the account Guard read itself, written before Guard acts on it) and `record_entry` (a forwarded entry once the venue reports the fill).
- **File names**: paper `risk-paper.jsonl` and `decisions-paper.jsonl`, testnet `risk.jsonl` and `decisions.jsonl`, mainnet `risk-mainnet.jsonl` and `decisions-mainnet.jsonl`, so a journal left from another mode in the same directory is never taken for this mode's (each risk journal is scoped to its network and account besides). Earlier builds named mainnet's journals as testnet's: `journal-init --mode mainnet` refuses while a `risk.jsonl` there is not plainly a testnet journal, and says to rename it (if it is this account's mainnet journal) or move it away.
- **Decision journal**: every event, one line `{"check": c, "prev": p, "durable": d, "event": e}`, `c` = SHA3-256 of `p` and the event's text, `d` the offset up to which the file was on disk when the line was written. One thread writes the file: it takes whatever is queued, writes it in order and syncs once for all of it. Every action Guard sends is named by an intent on disk first (a `decision`, `protect`, `flatten` or `intent` record: Guard waits for its sync, at most 2 s); what came back (`sent`), errors and risk events are synced with the next intent or within a second. The file grows in steps of 4 MiB of zeros, so that a sync writes data only; a clean stop gives the zeros back (while Guard runs, the file ends in zeros: read records through `/guard/events`, not `tail -f`). Opening checks every record; a changed, missing or reordered one before the last point known to be on disk is refused; what follows it (a crash's unsynced writes) is moved to `decisions*.jsonl.cut-<ms>` and recorded. On start, Guard asks the venue what became of every action an intent named that nothing answered (a crash, a send in doubt) and records `recovered` before serving. Every order Guard sends carries a client id (`0x7a68…` given to a bot's order without one, removed from the reply to the bot; `0x7a69…` Guard's own closes) so that the venue can be asked. A failed write or a sync over 2 s leaves the journal broken: nothing more is forwarded for a bot until a restart on an intact journal, while flattening and protection go on, each action recorded first in the **emergency log** (`emergency-decisions*.jsonl` in `emergency_dir` from `guard.toml`, default `<state_dir>/emergency`; best on another disk; a write that takes over 2 s lets the action go unrecorded, with an alert, and the log counts as stalled until that write finishes), with an alert. A writer that is only behind refuses bots' requests for a moment and is waited for by protection (the status counts both: `journal_dropped`, `journal_busy`). Recovery after a restart runs beside protection, at most 90 s, its reads within the request budget: `/healthz` says `recovering` and bots' requests are refused meanwhile; what it cannot read in time is `unknown`, with an alert. Vetoes are journaled 600 a minute at most for each client; `/guard/decision` answers recent decisions from memory.

The full terms are in the root `LICENSE`; `NOTICE` identifies the licensor and licence-key functionality. Third-party components retain their own licences in `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.md`.

- **The fee: 0.02% on every order Guard sends** (`f = 20` tenths of a basis point). Every order action Guard forwards or sends carries Orcastrate's Hyperliquid builder field `{"b": address, "f": 20}` while the account's approval is confirmed: the bot's entries with their TP/SL (one action, so the field covers the entry and its stops), its reduce-only closes, stops and take profits, Guard's own protective stops, the close Guard sends for a filled entry whose stop was refused, Guard's own closes, and every close of a flatten (kill switch, daily loss stop, drawdown halt). The venue charges the fee on each fill of such an order, a stop or take profit included when it fires. Trigger orders carry it since the testnet experiment of 6 Oct 2026 (`BUILDER_ON_TRIGGERS` in `licence.rs`, on): a standalone trigger resting with the field, and the stop of an entry that filled at once (a `normalTpsl` child already waiting for its trigger), still fire after the approval is withdrawn. **Two cases the experiment did not cover carry the field too** (`BUILDER_ON_RESTING_AND_POSITION_TPSL`, enabled): an entry that may rest (a limit other than IOC) sent with its stops, and a `positionTpsl` action. Should the venue refuse to activate such an entry's waiting stops when it fills after a withdrawal, or refuse a `positionTpsl` stop when it fires, the position is left without a stop, and Guard's protect pass covers it at its next protect pass: at the first sync once its last send has settled (5 s), or, while it keeps sending, once 30 s have passed since the pass before, at the sync interval (5 s by default, longer with HIP-3 dexes or a longer configured interval), and not while the account cannot be read. It places the stop a resting entry was sized for when that entry's fill explains the position (its side grew since it was sent by no more than its size: Guard follows each resting entry it forwarded, in memory, the newest 256, until it fills or is gone; a restart forgets them), unless the price is already through that stop; otherwise, as for any position without a stop, a stop at the default distance from the price, or a close when the price is through a stop the risk engine recorded; for a `positionTpsl` stop refused when it fires there is no recorded stop, so the new one goes at the default distance from the price then; first with the field (refused whole while Guard has not read the withdrawal) and at once again without it; tested in memory (`fee.rs`, `a_resting_entry_carries_the_field_and_a_refused_stop_is_covered_at_the_next_sync`, `a_position_tpsl_carries_the_field_and_a_refused_one_is_covered_at_the_next_sync`). These cases still require live testnet validation; in-memory tests do not establish venue behavior. A change by `modify` or `batchModify` is refused (`fee_not_approved`) while the approval is unconfirmed, since the venue cancels the old order before placing the replacement and its handling of a replacement that carries the field after a withdrawal is not tested (the order stays as it was; cancel it and send a new one, which goes without the field). That covers a withdrawal Guard has read; in the minutes before its next read (5 at most while approved), a modify goes out, and if the venue refuses it Guard protects the position at the next sync whatever else (a refused modify may have cancelled the order it was to replace) and reads the approval then. Changes to `BUILDER_ON_TRIGGERS` or `BUILDER_ON_RESTING_AND_POSITION_TPSL` require security review; a test in `licence.rs` (a `const` assertion, so it fails in the test build) fails if either constant changes unnoticed; tests switch them off through hooks behind the `test-hooks` feature (`FeeState::set_builder_on_triggers` and `set_builder_on_resting_and_position_tpsl`, and for a whole Guard `Guard::set_builder_on_triggers`), which only the test build enables (a dev-dependency of `zunder-guard` on itself), never a release build. The address is a compiled-in constant per network (`licence.rs`): `ORCASTRATE_BUILDER` for mainnet, `ORCASTRATE_TESTNET_BUILDER` for testnet; the mainnet builder is set (`0x0f50112710913B51A5D037795e5F4EFc08deBf2a`, 6 Oct 2026; the venue accepts it only with at least 100 USDC of perps account value, and until then refuses the field, which Guard reports as the builder's side), the testnet one is `None`, so testnet stays "off: testnet". The decision journal shows the field as it goes out; an action sent again without it is journaled in its `sent` event (`action`).
- **No builder field, by the venue's design:** `modify` and `batchModify` (Hyperliquid's modify carries an order without a builder field; the venue keeps the original order's builder through a modify, tested on testnet on 6 Oct 2026, so a modified stop still pays), `cancel`, `cancelByCloid`, `scheduleCancel`, `updateLeverage` and `updateIsolatedMargin` (not orders). Guard never forwards TWAP, spot or vault orders. A trigger modify rests the order under a **new oid with the same client id**, so Guard knows its own stops by client id (`0x7a67…`), never by a remembered oid: it reads the open orders fresh for every decision, and cancels its own stop, when a bot's tighter stop replaces it, by the oid of that fresh read (the venue's look-up by client id was seen to answer for the old oid after a modify); a cancel that fails is an alert. A modify of Guard's stop keeps its client id (Guard adds it when the modify names none, and refuses one that names another), and no other order may take one of Guard's ids.
- **Approval.** The user approves the fee once with the account's main wallet (`approveBuilderFee`, a user-signed action; the page is https://zunderlabs.com/approve); Guard never sends or forwards `approveBuilderFee` or `setReferrer` (`funds_or_permissions`). Guard reads the approval with the `/info` request `{"type": "maxBuilderFee", "user": <account>, "builder": <builder>}` (the answer is the approved maximum in tenths of a bp; weight 20) at the end of the background sync, outside the request lock and with a 2.5 s timeout, and only after the sync read the account (a venue that cannot answer that gets no further request): at the first sync, then every 5 minutes while approved and every minute while not, and at the next sync when an entry was refused for it (at most every 15 s, from the request budget), so an approval given meanwhile counts within one sync. An unreadable answer keeps what Guard knew. Hyperliquid refuses an order whose builder the user has not approved ("Builder fee has not been approved."), so:
  - **New entries** are refused with `fee_not_approved` while the approval is unconfirmed (not read yet, below 20, withdrawn, or refused by the venue), with the reason in the text and the status (`entries_blocked`, an alert). Nothing is sent.
  - **Guard never holds an exit back for the fee.** While the approval is unconfirmed, closes, stops and flattens go without the field. When the venue refuses an exit for its field (the approval was withdrawn since the last read) and placed none of its orders, Guard sends the same exit again at once without the field (one more round trip); an action of which the venue placed some orders is never sent again. An entry the venue refuses for the field is answered `fee_not_approved` and not sent again.
  - **A refusal by the venue** carries the venue's text into the alert and the status (`approval.venue`). Only the known builder-side text ("insufficient balance") is Orcastrate's to fix (`refused_by_venue_builder`): it says so, shows no approval page, and holds until the approval Guard reads changes or 10 minutes have passed. Any other text is taken as the account's approval (`refused_by_venue`, with the approval page), and the next read ends it, so an approval given again counts at once. Entries stay refused meanwhile.
  - **A withdrawal** (found by a check or by a refusal) is an alert. Orders already resting with the field are left as they are: the venue cancels none of them on a withdrawal, and a standalone stop resting with the field, or the stop of an entry that filled at once, still fires (and pays) after it (testnet, 6 Oct 2026), so nothing is placed again. New orders go without the field until the approval is confirmed again. The checks run in order: an answer to an older check than one recorded is ignored, one check at a time, and none on a kill (its reply does not wait).
  - **Sizing.** An entry is sized with the builder fee counted on both sides (`fee_bps` 4.5 + 2 = 6.5 bp per side by default; `sizing_fee_bps` in the status), so a stop-out still loses no more than the budget. This only makes sizes smaller.
  - **Paper mode** reports the fee it would charge on the network whose account it reads (and that account's approval), in the status and in each paper reply (`builder_fee`: address, rate, the entry's fee at its worst price, whether approved). It never blocks.
  - Approval changes are journaled as `error` events (event schema 1 has no event of its own for them).
- **A client's builder field** (ccxt adds its own) is refused on an entry (`client_builder`), whatever it names (Guard's own address at another fee included), and removed from an exit, which is never refused for it (a change in the decision); Guard replaces the field of every order action it sends with its own (or none), so no other builder can reach the venue.
- **The licence key** is licence-key functionality, kept in one module (`licence.rs`): `zgl1_` + base64url(payload) + `.` + base64url(ed25519 signature over the payload's bytes), payload `{"licensee", "expires_at_ms", "features": ["fee_free"], "accounts": ["0x…"], "builder"?: {"address", "fee_tenths_bp"}}`, verified offline against a public key compiled in (`LICENCE_PUBLIC_KEY_HEX`, 64 hex digits, decoded at compile time so a malformed constant fails the build; Orcastrate's production key since 6 Oct 2026). Nothing phones home. `fee_free` runs without the builder field on every network, and Guard then reads no approval; a licence may name another builder (mainnet only). A key is good only for the accounts it names: Guard's own account must be one of them, and a key that names none (every key issued before 7 Oct 2026) is good for none. A missing, malformed, forged or expired key, or one for other accounts, never blocks trading: Guard falls back to the fee and warns at start and in the event stream (red team: `redteam_a_licence_for_another_account_buys_nothing`). Tests sign with `licence::test_key` (a fixed seed in the source, so it proves nothing); a test fails if the production constant is ever that key.
- **The licence's lifecycle** (`licence.rs`, "Lifecycle"; `licence_life.rs`). `zunder-guard licence set zgl1_…` checks the key for the configured account (refused, config unchanged, if it does not verify, has ended or is for other accounts), writes it into `guard.toml` and waits for the running Guard to report it; `init --licence` (and the installers' `--licence`, `-Licence` on Windows) does the same at setup; `licence show` describes the key. A running Guard reads and validates `guard.toml` at every sync and applies only a changed `licence`, without a restart; equal-length updates with the same modification time are read too; every other setting still needs one. It checks the key's end before every order and at every sync: an alert (and standard error) 14, 7 and 1 days before, one at a time and kept while it holds (other alerts go after an hour), and from the first order after the end the builder fee (entries then wait for the fee's approval, which Guard reads at once; exits never wait), journaled. The status carries `licence` (`state`: `active`, `not_used` or `none`; `licensee`, `expires_at_ms`, `days_left`, `error`, `auto_update`). **Automatic renewal is off by default**, so nothing phones home: with `licence_auto_update = true` and `licence_renewal_token` (from the renewal link in the licence email), Guard asks `https://zunderlabs.com/api/licence/renewal` (the token and the account address) for the licence's newest key from 14 days before the end, or while the key is missing or unusable, at most every 6 hours, and writes it into `guard.toml` only if it verifies offline for the account and ends later than the one there; the sync then applies it. Tests: `fee.rs` (`a_licence_applies_live_warns_before_it_ends_and_falls_back_at_the_next_order`, `a_bad_new_licence_key_or_an_unreadable_config_changes_nothing`), `licence_life.rs`, `init.rs` (`init_writes_a_licence_key_only_for_this_account`).

## Compatibility

Checked in the tools' source on 6 Oct 2026 (ccxt `master`, Freqtrade `develop`, `hyperliquid-python-sdk` `master`, `@nktkas/hyperliquid` `main`; all MIT or Apache-2.0). None of them checks that the signing key matches the account address, so a Guard client key works unchanged. Line numbers are approximate.

**ccxt** (`ts/src/hyperliquid.ts`, `ts/src/pro/hyperliquid.ts`; Python is the same). The REST URLs are `urls.api.public`/`private`, with `urls.test` for the sandbox (around L142-151); `sandbox: true` copies `urls.test` over `urls.api` (base `Exchange.ts`); requests go to `apiUrl + '/' + path`. The phantom agent comes from `options.sandboxMode` (`constructPhantomAgent`, around L1697-1725), so set `sandbox: true` and override `urls.test`. `walletAddress` is only the `user` of info queries; everything is signed with `privateKey`. ccxt sends `approveBuilderFee` and a one-time `setReferrer` before trading (`initializeClient`, around L1915) and adds its own `builder` to orders once approved: Guard refuses all three, so set `builderFee: false` and `refSet: true`, and never set `approvedBuilderFee: true`: ccxt then takes the approval as given and attaches its own `builder` field to orders, which Guard refuses (`client_builder` on entries; stripped from exits). Market orders are IOC limits at the mid ± `defaultSlippage` (5%), which Guard pulls in to its 0.5% bound; `stopLoss`/`takeProfit` params produce a `normalTpsl` group, which Guard keeps.

```python
ccxt.hyperliquid({
    "walletAddress": "0xYourAccount",          # the main wallet's address
    "privateKey": "0xClientKeyFromGuardInit",  # Guard's client key, never the API wallet's
    "sandbox": True,                           # signs as testnet; Guard accepts either
    "urls": {"test": {"public": "http://127.0.0.1:8547", "private": "http://127.0.0.1:8547",
                      "ws": {"public": "ws://127.0.0.1:8547/ws"}}},
    "options": {"builderFee": False, "refSet": True},  # never approvedBuilderFee: True
})
```

**Freqtrade** (`freqtrade/exchange/hyperliquid.py`, `exchange.py`, `docs/exchanges.md`): `walletAddress` and `privateKey` go straight to ccxt (around L413-426); `ccxt_config` applies to both clients, `ccxt_async_config` on top (around L279-299). Use `margin_mode: "isolated"`: Freqtrade sends `updateLeverage` before every entry and every stop (`_lev_prep`), and Guard refuses cross margin. Stoploss on exchange is a reduce-only stop-limit trigger in its own request after the fill (`create_stoploss`, around L1619-1690): Guard has attached its own market stop to the entry by then and keeps it; Freqtrade's stop-limit rests beside it but never replaces it, since a stop-limit may not fill. Freqtrade's later stop moves (cancel and replace of its own stop-limit) pass.

## Latency

The CPU time Guard adds to a request before it leaves the machine, release build on the Linux build host (`cargo test --release -p zunder-guard-core latency_of_the_full_request_path -- --ignored --nocapture`, 20,000 ccxt-style market buys with an attached stop):

| Step | p50 | p99 | p99.9 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decode and authenticate (one signer recovery) | 87.8 µs | 92.5 µs | 97.4 µs |
| Judge (risk engine sizing, attached stop, isolated leverage) | 5.1 µs | 5.4 µs | 9.2 µs |
| Re-sign (two signatures: the leverage update and the order) | 145.1 µs | 150.2 µs | 164.0 µs |
| **Total** | **238.2 µs** | **244.1 µs** | **258.3 µs** |

Measured 6 Oct 2026 on the Linux build host (16 vCPUs). A client signing as mainnet costs a second recovery (about 80 µs more); an order on a position that is already open needs one signature, not two.

Not included: the account (from the stream, or one round trip to Hyperliquid to read it: below), reading the leverage back, and sending (one round trip, two when isolated leverage is set first).

**End to end, in paper mode** (bot to Guard to its would-send answer, over HTTP on loopback; `crates/zunder-guard/tests/latency_live.rs`, release build on the Linux build host in Frankfurt against testnet, 6 Oct 2026, with the snapshots revalued at fresh marks; 300 small BTC buys one every 300 ms with the stream warm and the background sync running):

## Deposits and withdrawals

Guard keeps deposits, withdrawals and transfers out of its account stops: a withdrawal is no loss, a deposit hides no loss, and trading losses count in full. The implementation is in `crates/zunder-venue/src/flows.rs`; its independent reference model is in `crates/zunder-venue/tests/flows_model.rs`.

- **Where the flows come from.** The account's non-funding ledger: the stream's `userNonFundingLedgerUpdates` as entries arrive; over HTTP (`userNonFundingLedgerUpdates`, weight 20 and 1 per 20 entries, up to 4 pages of 500, all within 3 s, since the read runs under Guard's lock) at the first observation (from 30 minutes before the risk journal's horizon, so that a flow reported late or not yet applied when Guard stopped comes again), before any view that would halt or stop the risk engine unless a complete read began after it and at least 2 s after the first such view (at most once a second, every 3 s after a failure), every 5 minutes while the stream is connected (an entry it missed), and every minute while it is not or after it reconnected. Each read starts 60 s before where the last complete one ended, so that an entry the venue showed late is not missed; a flow seen again is recognised by its id. A read that stopped at the fourth full page counts as not done. Which entries count, and for how much, is `zunder_guard_core::ledger::ledger_flow`: the USDC perpetual accounts of every dex Guard manages (a `send` between two of them is only its fee, and marked as a transfer between them).
- **What a flow does.** The peak and the day's start move so that a loss stays the same share of them as the risk engine measures it: in proportion to the equity without an equity cap, or below it; by the amount at or above the cap, where the engine measures a loss in dollars against the cap. A deposit on a day that is up keeps that day's gain in dollars (it is not scaled up). 1,000 withdrawn at the peak of 10,000: peak and day start 9,000. At 9,500 (5% down), 4,750 withdrawn: 5,000 and 5,000, still 5% down, and 100 more lost halts the day. With a cap of 2,000: 110 lost from 10,000 (5.5% of the cap), 7,890 withdrawn: the day's start becomes 2,110, and 97 more lost halts the day (207 of 2,000). Moving money out and back leaves everything as it was.
- **When it is applied.** At a fresh view whose relevant dex venue times show it at least 2 s after its ledger time. Views within the margin are omitted; stale/equal venue times cannot end or deepen a hold. Complete fills, signed anchor position values, signed total fees/rebates inclusive of builder fees, funding and ledger movements reconstruct exact pre-flow cash only if every individual position was flat then. Current positions may differ. No trade candles or historical price estimate enter a decision.
- **When it is unknown.** Positions open at the transfer time, incomplete/malformed/truncated records or unsupported collateral cause a day halt and alert. Exact losses already proved before that gap remain; known money movements and UTC rolls are applied without guessing when a trading loss happened. The next trusted cash checkpoint or showing view normalizes the retained bases; later separately proved trading losses count normally and may stop Guard. A deposit never masks a proved loss. Historical ranges, including singletons, without explicit cash-exact provenance are unknown. Optional history reads share the existing `/info` budget; if it is busy or scaled down, reconstruction can also be unknown and halt the day. This can halt users who transfer with open positions even if their account rose. Reducing positions, exits and protection continue; no existing human stop resumes automatically.
- **Reported late.** A deposit seen in the equity first, as a gain, or a withdrawal seen first as a loss, is placed at its own time when its entry arrives, and the engine is recomputed from the last journal record before it with every view since: its figures are then what they would have been, and a loss the deposit hid halts it then. A halt or drawdown stop taken meanwhile stays. Before a view that would halt or stop the engine, Guard reads the ledger, and counts the view once a complete read that began after it, and at least 2 s after the first of the views in a row that would halt, shows no flow (the ledger may come from a node a little behind the account's); meanwhile, and while the ledger cannot be read, the view is held back for at most 10 s, counted from the first such view; when a view comes that would not halt (the loss went: it was trading, since a withdrawal does not come back), or such a read succeeds, or the 10 s are over, the deepest view held is counted first (protection and flattening go on from the sync; Guard looks again every second for the whole hold, including one begun by an order request; stale snapshots cannot clear it, and wall time expires it even after account-read failure). No new positions meanwhile (`account_unreadable`), nor while no view has been taken for 10 s (each may or may not show a flow, or is older than one before it: a venue time that does not move or goes back).
- **Empty accounts and pending money.** A nonpositive account view can defer a flow group until a fresh positive view/refill. Exact losses already proved are retained; the original monetary checkpoint and pending history stay separate from the displayed current day and survive restart and normal retention limits. Entries remain blocked while flows are pending, including after midnight; exits and protection continue. Once a fresh view can fully settle the group, its money applies once. Extreme Decimal arithmetic can also leave an exact group pending; refill does not necessarily resolve an earlier unrepresentable checkpoint. If retained history reaches its hard capacity bound, Guard requires journal repair/review instead of dropping that history. The ordinary resume command refuses while money is pending and does not resolve it; compare the journal with the account history before any manual repair. Retained pending views are repeated in each durable record, so prolonged pending periods grow journal bytes and replay work quadratically. Monitor disk space and reconcile prolonged pending history; write failure blocks entries. Incremental pending-journal storage is separate work.
- **On the journal.** Every record of Guard's risk engine is version 3 and carries the latest venue time it has seen of each dex; a fall of the last equity by more than 0.1% of the day's start is written too. Each flow is a `flowed` record from an earlier record (its base) with every view and every flow since, and its state is what the shared function (`crates/zunder-venue/src/flows.rs`) computes from them; the reader computes the same and refuses the record otherwise, and checks the base and the lists against the records before. A restart starts from the last record and knows every flow already applied. A flow older than every record a recomputation can start from (a day, or a review since), or from before the journal was started (`zunder-guard init`: it is in the equity the journal started from), is not applied and counts as the gain or loss it already was (`risk.flow_note` says so); `risk.flows_applied` counts the flows applied.
- **Opt-in accounting.** Flow adjustment is enabled per engine with `PersistentRisk::with_flows`. Callers that leave it disabled retain the original risk-engine behavior and version-2 journal format.

## The account stream

Guard follows the account over Hyperliquid's WebSocket (`crates/zunder-guard/src/stream.rs`), started with `run`, so that a request is usually judged without a round trip to the venue. Measured on mainnet and testnet, 6 Oct 2026 (public data, no key):

| Channel | What it carries | How often |
|---|---|---|
| `clearinghouseState`, `openOrders` (each with `user`, `dex`), `allMids` (`dex`) | whole snapshots, in the shape of the `info` answers (`openOrders` as `frontendOpenOrders`); `clearinghouseState` has the venue's `time` | every 5 s (5.15 s apart at most), changed or not; computed 0.5 to 0.8 s before they arrive |
| `activeAssetCtx` (per coin) | the coin's mark (`markPx`) and mid (`midPx`) | every 1.02 s at the median, 1.45 s at most, changed or not |
| `bbo` (per coin) | best bid and ask, with the venue's `time` | at each block in which they change: every 0.1 s on BTC, nothing for up to 12 s on a quiet HIP-3 coin |
| `l2Book` (per coin) | the book | every 5.4 s, 20 levels a side; with `"fast": true` (the venue echoes it; it is not in the documentation) every 0.54 s, changed or not, but 5 levels a side. Guard follows no book |
| `orderUpdates`, `userEvents` (channel `user`), `userNonFundingLedgerUpdates` | events: orders, fills, funding, liquidations, cancels by the venue, transfers | as they happen, 0.2 to 0.9 s after the venue's time |

Every subscription is answered by a `subscriptionResponse` echoing it (an unsubscription too, after which nothing more of it arrives). An unknown coin, a malformed or a repeated subscription is answered by an `error` (`Invalid subscription`, `Already subscribed`); an unknown dex by nothing at all. The venue's times trail the wall clock by 0.45 to 1.1 s on `clearinghouseState` and 0.3 to 0.4 s on `bbo`.

Guard subscribes to the event channels first, then each dex's three snapshots (the main dex and every HIP-3 dex the markets name), and, for every coin with a position or an open order and every coin it judged an entry in (32 at most; coins with positions or orders are never dropped), to its `activeAssetCtx` and `bbo` (never a spot coin, `@…` or `…/USDC`, nor an outcome, `#…`: they have no perp context). A HIP-3 entry's book, for the thin-book cap, is read over HTTP for the entry (`l2Book`, 20 levels, weight 2, within the request-read budget) on either path: the fast book's 5 levels often do not reach a stop's worst fill.

**Clean.** A request is judged from the stream only when:

- the socket is up, no `error` came on it, and the venue confirmed every subscription the view needs;
- every snapshot of every dex arrived within 6 s, and each `clearinghouseState` is within 6 s of the newest venue time seen on the socket;
- every snapshot arrived at least 1 s after the account's last event and after Guard last heard back from the venue on something it sent, and its `time` is not before the last event's;
- no `clearinghouseState` went back in time since the socket connected;
- every coin with a position or an order, and the entry's coin, has a mark and a mid (the newer of `bbo` and `activeAssetCtx`) that arrived within 1.5 s, the mid within 5% of the snapshot's `allMids` (a bigger move within one snapshot period is read over HTTP);
- the venue's time on the last timed message (`clearinghouseState`, `bbo`), and on each dex's `clearinghouseState`, trailed the wall clock by at most 3 s when it arrived: a WebSocket server whose node lags is not used. This leans on the host's clock: one running X s slow hides a server X s behind (a host clock kept by NTP, as on AWS, is off by milliseconds);
- the reference data Guard read over HTTP (`meta`, the account mode, `perpDexs`, each HIP-3 dex's `meta` and caps) is within its lifetime (no older than the HTTP path allows).

Ages are measured on a monotonic clock, not the wall clock.

**Revalued.** The snapshot is then revalued at those marks: the venue values a position at its mark (`positionValue` is `|szi| × markPx`, checked against testnet's answers), so a mark move since the snapshot changes the account value by `szi × markPx_now − sign(szi) × positionValue` per position, exactly the venue's own arithmetic. The equity the judge, the daily loss stop and the drawdown stop see is that revalued one; `withdrawable` (the HIP-3 margin cap) is lowered by twice the sum of the moves and never raised; the mids of the coins above are the fresh ones (open risk is measured from them). Otherwise the request reads the account over HTTP exactly as before (and an unreadable account still lets reduce-only orders through unjudged): exits never wait for the stream.

**How fresh each input is**, on each path (mainnet from Frankfurt; testnet's API answers in 0.46 to 0.69 s):

| Input | Read over HTTP | From the stream |
|---|---|---|
| Positions, orders, triggers, leverage, liquidation prices | the read's: one round trip old (about 0.24 s) | the last snapshot's: arrived within 6 s and computed up to 0.8 s before. A change that raises an event and was made more than about 0.9 s before the judgement is in it (events arrive 0.2 to 0.9 s after the venue's time, and snapshots older than 1 s after the last event are not used); one made within the last 0.9 s can be missing, where a read is about 0.24 s old. Changes that raise no event (below) can be up to about 6.8 s old |
| Equity (account value), daily and drawdown stops | the read's, at the venue's marks | the snapshot revalued at marks that arrived within 1.5 s (computed about 0.3 s before) |
| `withdrawable` (HIP-3 margin cap) | the read's | the snapshot's, lowered by the mark moves, never raised |
| Mid of the entry's coin and of every coin with a position or order (open risk, worst price, closes) | the read's `allMids` | `bbo` or `activeAssetCtx`, arrived within 1.5 s, within 5% of `allMids` |
| Other coins' mids | the read's `allMids` | the snapshot's `allMids` (no judgement uses them) |
| A HIP-3 entry's book (thin-book cap) | read for the request (20 levels) | read for the request (20 levels) |
| `meta`, account mode, caps; `perpDexs` | cached up to 60 s; 10 min | the same caches, within the same lifetimes |
| Flattening on a halt or the kill switch | a fresh read (the sync's, or the request's own) | never from the stream: a request that finds Guard halted reads the account over HTTP and flattens from that, at most once every 3 s, within the request-read budget, and not when neither the stream nor the last read showed anything to flatten; otherwise the sync flattens at its next round |

**What raises no event.** Measured: orders placed, cancelled and filled raise `orderUpdates`; fills `userEvents`; the ledger channel opens with its history marked `isSnapshot`, which Guard does not count as a change. Per Hyperliquid's documentation (not verified here: it needs a signed action on testnet), funding payments, liquidations and the venue's own cancels come on `userEvents`, and deposits, withdrawals, transfers (between spot and perps, between dexes, sub-accounts, vaults) on `userNonFundingLedgerUpdates`. By the same documentation none of these channels announces a leverage change (`updateLeverage`), an isolated margin change (`updateIsolatedMargin`) or an account mode change, nor (unverified either way) a HIP-3 deployer's halt or settlement. Guard's own such actions count as its sends; one made elsewhere (the web app, another bot on the account) is seen at the next snapshot, up to about 6.8 s later. The leverage setting itself also comes on `activeAssetData`, every 5 s (more often while the account's figures move, about every second on an account with positions). Guard skips setting isolated leverage before an entry only on a setting that arrived within 1.5 s, so it saves the update only part of the time (about 30% on an account without positions), and a change made elsewhere in the last 1.5 s plus the venue's delay can be missed: the entry then goes at the setting made elsewhere, cross or a higher isolated leverage, whose liquidation may lie inside the stop (before this, Guard set isolated leverage before every new position). A coin left at a low isolated setting (1x, say) is kept, so an entry can then be refused for margin by the venue, intermittently, where setting the higher leverage first would have let it through; the account mode is cached for a minute on either path, and a halted HIP-3 market shows in its dex's `meta`, cached for a minute on either path too.

The status's `stream` says whether the socket is up, the coins followed, how many requests were judged each way and why the last could not use the stream. The socket is pinged every 20 s, and reconnected (1 s, doubling to 30 s) when it drops or is silent for 10 s; when it brings an `error` or a snapshot out of order it is reconnected too, but after connections broken that way the wait keeps doubling, to 5 min, and from the second in a row the status carries an alert (`stream.state.breaks`, `stream.state.last_break`). Everything it knew is dropped on a reconnect.

**The bot's own reads.** While the stream is clean, a bot's `clearinghouseState` and `frontendOpenOrders` of Guard's account are answered from it (the latest snapshot as the venue sent it, as its WebSocket would send it to the bot; at most 20 a second), so a bot behind Guard spends less of the IP's request weight than without it. Everything else, another account's reads, `openOrders`, `allMids` (a snapshot up to 6 s old would be worse than the venue's 0.5 s cache), and every read while the stream is not clean, goes to the venue as before.

**Staying clean after Guard's own sends.** Before, after a send the stream waited for a snapshot that arrived at least 1 s after Guard heard back (up to about 6 s on mainnet), so an active bot's next requests were judged after a read. Now every send Guard makes is recorded with the stream (before it counts as Guard's last send), with what the venue's answer says the socket will report of it, when Guard can tell: for an order action on one dex, the ids that rested or filled and the client ids it sent (a `normalTpsl` entry's waiting stop is reported `open` under Guard's `0x7a67` client id, with an id the answer does not name); for a cancel by id that worked, the ids it took, the children the view lists under them, and the stops Guard attached to a resting entry (the venue lists those as orders of their own and cancels them with the entry; by client id). The recording on testnet (an entry resting with its stop, a cancel, a filled entry, a close) showed the shapes: fills on `user` first, then `orderUpdates` with the id, client id, status and `statusTimestamp`; the dex's `openOrders` and `clearinghouseState` after them, in the same batch for a cancel or a close, 0.6 to 1.4 s later for an entry (perhaps only the next periodic one; one of each was recorded), the account's `time` at or after the events'. An event is a send's own when its id and status match (or a listed client id comes `open`, or comes `canceled` for an attached stop, or a reduce-only order resting on a coin the send filled on comes `reduceOnlyCanceled`, as the venue cancels those when a fill empties the position) within 5 s of the send. The stream is clean after Guard's sends when, for every recent send: either every listed order was reported, and the send's dex (and for a HIP-3 dex the main dex too, whose margin it may draw on) has an `openOrders` and a `clearinghouseState` that came after those reports and next to each other among that dex's snapshots, the account's venue `time` not before them; or, for any other send, the old rule (snapshots at least 1 s after it). The old rule applies to: any event no send explains (another app's order, a fill of a resting order or a stop later, a funding payment, a transfer), a send whose outcome is unknown (no answer, an HTTP error), an answer whose reports do not all come, orders on more than one dex, a modify, a cancel by client id, and a leverage or margin update (no event): Guard's own `updateLeverage` before an entry keeps the leverage setting shown before it out, and the view unclean, for 1 s. The view itself is still the venue's snapshot revalued at fresh marks; nothing is applied to it from events. Measured with the in-memory venue at the venue's cadence as recorded (snapshots every 5 s, 1 s after an entry's events or only at the next periodic one, at once after a cancel's or a close's; prices every 0.5 s) and the production budget, a bot sending an entry every 250 ms gets 51 entries a minute through on its own account, and 25 to 26 with another app's order every 2 s, the same whether the venue's snapshot after an entry comes 1 s later or only with the next periodic one; median latency 11.6 to 12.1 ms (in-memory, a Linux build host at load 4 to 13). For comparison only, not like for like: before this change, with an earlier in-memory venue that sent only periodic snapshots, 27 and 25. The gain is the 1 s wait after each send; the snapshot after an entry still takes its time.

**The background sync** goes on reading the account over HTTP on its own schedule: the periodic reconciliation, protection, and flattening come from it. It reads without Guard's lock, so requests do not wait for its round trips. When Guard heard back on a send within the last 2 s (a bot that is sending will likely send again), it reads holding the lock at once: one read. Otherwise, should Guard send anything meanwhile (it counts its sends), its read may predate the send: it reads again holding the lock (up to 5 s, then the round's protection or flattening sends), finishes the round from that read, and waits one interval longer before its next round, so protection (the 30 s backstop, the protection due after a cancelled entry that may have filled in part, flattening on a failed read) runs every round however often a bot sends. Two overlapping syncs (the periodic one and a kill's) take the newer of their cached reference data. The risk engine is shown a sync's read when it is newer than the view it saw last (a request's stream view counts as old as its oldest snapshot), never an older one after a newer, and its observations are stamped in time order. While a request reads the account over HTTP it holds the lock, each venue call bounded only by the transport's 15 s timeout; a halted request reads up to 5 s, 5 s more for the positions alone if that fails, then sends the closes; the sync's read under the lock is bounded at 5 s. A view's settle window counts from when it was read, not when the risk engine is shown it.

## Unsupported features

Spot and HIP-4 outcome markets; HIP-3 markets on mainnet, more than two HIP-3 dexes, HIP-3 dexes margined in another token than USDC, HIP-3 entries through the agent kit (it reads the main dex's `meta` only); trigger entries; more than one entry per action; modifying a resting entry; removing isolated margin; adding to a position on cross margin; account modes other than standard; vaults and sub-accounts; TWAP; accepting a new client without a restart. The browser Guard, the relay and pairing.
